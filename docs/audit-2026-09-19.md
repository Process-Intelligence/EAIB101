## 7. Findings and recommendations

### Student pages — what the numbers in §2 and §3 mean

**The class agrees on the mechanics and disagrees on the numbers.** All 34 pages subtract social insurance before computing tax, 33 of 34 never let tax go below zero, and 27 recalculate as you type. Where they diverge is exactly where Step 1 of the assignment sends students to the law:

- **Contribution ceiling.** 23 pages cap the contribution base at 7,920,000 ₮ (792,000 × 10). Six use 6,600,000 ₮ (660,000 × 10 — the previous year's minimum wage, sometimes with the accompanying "max 759,000 ₮" text), two use an editable default of 7,700,000 ₮, and three have no ceiling at all. This is a single fact with a date attached, and a third of the class has it wrong or missing.
- **Employee rate.** 28 pages withhold 11.5%; six withhold 9.5%, i.e. the social-insurance rate without the 2% health-insurance component.
- **Tax bands.** 18 pages apply monthly bands, 10 annualise (× 12 against 120M/180M, ÷ 12), and six apply a flat 10% with no bands. The monthly and annualised forms are equivalent when the thresholds are 10M/15M and 120M/180M; two monthly pages instead use 10M/20M/30M with a 25% top rate, and one zero-rates the first 792,000 ₮ of monthly taxable income with no comment or source.
- **The credit is where the class scatters most: 16 distinct schedules across 34 pages.** Roughly a third key a tapering credit on monthly taxable income (20k → 10k → 0 between 0.5M and 3M) or on annualised taxable (240k → 120k → 0 between 6M and 36M ÷ 12) — two ways of writing the same rule. The rest use a flat amount (24,000 ₮ on two pages, 20,000 on three, 11,000, 10,000, and one page a monthly 79,200), tiers keyed on **gross** rather than taxable income, tiers that never reach zero, tiers with a step missing, a user-entered box defaulting to 0, or no credit at all while the prose claims one. Because the credit is applied after tax, most of these errors change net pay by only 10–30 thousand ₮ a month — small enough that the page looks right and only a hand calculation catches it, which is the point of the "calculate these by hand first" requirement.

**Requirements coverage.**

- *Six labelled output lines:* 24 of 34 show all six. The lines most often missing are exactly the two the assignment exists to make visible — tax before credit (10 pages) and credit applied (9) — with taxable income missing on five.
- *Five test cases:* eight pages compute them on the page, two hard-code a static table, two mix, three offer preset buttons with no expected values, and **19 show none**. Only two pages cover all five required kinds. Both static tables disagree with the page's own code — **B26FA1820** (its "tax comes to zero" row shows a credit its own JavaScript caps lower) and **B26FA1900** (four of eight rows differ) — the two high-severity computation defects in the set. A static table cannot fail, which is why the assignment asks for one that can.
- *Law, article and date in the code:* 13 pages name articles in code comments; only four record a date checked; 13 carry no citation anywhere. "Every rate carries a comment naming the law, the article and the date you checked it" is the least-met requirement.
- *Step 3 (Prompt A vs B and the six-sentence conclusion):* on the page for four students. If it was submitted separately, this is not a gap.
- *Step 4:* 25 pages add at least some payslip fields; 18 have seven or more of the nine (days, hours, overtime, night, rest day, holiday, bonus, breakdown, new hand cases); nine pages stop at the original calculator. Recurring Step 4 issues: rest-day and holiday premiums coded as 2.0× where the label says 1.5×, night premium coded as ×1.2 on pages whose label says +20% (equivalent only if the base hour is also paid), and a "days worked" proration that leaves the hourly rate on the full month.
- *Self-contained:* 31 of 34. **B26FA1838** takes all of its styling from `cdn.tailwindcss.com` and renders as unstyled text offline (high); three pages link Google webfonts with fallbacks declared (low).

**Hygiene.** Four pages carry leftover AI citation markers (`[cite: 1]`, `citeturn0search5`) in their visible text — direct evidence of unchecked paste on an assignment about checking. One page has two doctypes; one arrived with a stray shell command above the doctype that was removed at the instructor's decision before publishing. Three pairs are byte-identical (1822/1836, 1827/1833, 1832/1835), as recorded when they were integrated.

**Reference values — to be confirmed, not taken from here.** The assignment forbids taking rates from an AI, and this report does not ask the reader to. For orientation only, the position most of the class converged on, and what I understand the 2026 rules to be: employee withholding 11.5% (social insurance 9.5% + health insurance 2%), base capped at ten times the monthly minimum wage (792,000 → 7,920,000 ₮), income tax on annual taxable income at 10% to 120M, 15% to 180M, 20% above (monthly equivalents 10M/15M), and a credit that tapers from 240,000 ₮ a year (20,000 a month) to zero above 36M annual taxable income. Verify each on legalinfo.mn before using it to grade.

## 8. Method and caveats

- **Runtime check.** Each page's inline script was executed in a Node `vm` sandbox with a stub DOM (elements seeded from the markup, browser-style named globals, `Intl`, `insertAdjacentHTML`, id selectors). It catches thrown errors, references to elements absent from the markup, and empty outputs; it cannot resolve descendant or class selectors, so one page (B26FA1894, `querySelector("#testTable tbody")`) reports a harness error that is not a page bug.
- **No legal ground truth was injected.** Auditors were told to record what each page's JavaScript does, not to grade it against the law. The distributions in §2 show where the class disagrees; deciding which value is right is the instructor's call and is the point of Step 1 of the assignment.
- **Identical pairs** were audited as one page and reported twice.
- Student pages, `students.json` and `problem-sets.json` were read only.
